In the harsh winter of 1888, the mountain settlement of Blackwood Ridge faced an unprecedented isolation. Heavy blizzards had collapsed the wooden rail trestle across the eastern gorge, cutting off coal and grain shipments. In the central hall, Mayor Thomas Vance convened an emergency council of locomotive engineers and master carpenters. They drafted plans to construct a reinforced timber trestle despite sub-zero winds. Within forty-eight hours, crews hoisted massive cedar beams into place using steam winches and hemp ropes. By dawn on the fourth day, the first relief locomotive crested the ridge, its brass whistle echoing across the snowbound valley.
