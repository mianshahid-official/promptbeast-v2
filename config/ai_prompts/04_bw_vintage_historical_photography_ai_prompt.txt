STYLE DIRECTIVES: BLACK & WHITE VINTAGE HISTORICAL PHOTOGRAPHY
================================================================================

1. GLOBAL VISUAL FOUNDATION:
- An authentic black-and-white historical documentary photography aesthetic. Every image must look like a genuine photograph captured with historical camera equipment and preserved in a museum or national archive.
- Authentic monochrome photographic rendering with natural grayscale tonal variation, realistic contrast, organic photographic grain, and period-appropriate sharpness.
- It must NEVER look like a modern color digital photograph cheaply desaturated to grayscale; it must exhibit genuine optical depth, authentic silver halides, and tonal richness.
- AVOID: Colorization, selective color accents, digital illustration, cartoon aesthetics, 3D CGI rendering, artificial-looking plastic skin, exaggerated cinematic glow, and repetitive heavy digital damage filters.

2. HUMAN SUBJECTS & ANATOMY:
- Humans are realistic photographic subjects with natural anatomy, authentic facial features, realistic proportions, and genuine expressions.
- Real individuals captured by a camera lens—never CGI or illustrated.
- Clothing, hairstyles, uniforms, and headwear must strictly reflect the historical period and geography specified in the script.

3. ANIMALS, BIRDS & OBJECTS:
- Animals and birds are rendered with realistic species-specific anatomy, authentic fur/feather textures, natural poses, and genuine interactions with their surroundings.
- Architecture, vehicles, ships, locomotives, weapons, and tools must precisely reflect the engineering and design of the target historical period.

4. SCRIPT-DRIVEN DIVERSITY:
- Lighting, exposure, camera angle, focal length, depth of field, and framing must adapt dynamically to each scene's narrative emotional tone (e.g. harsh midday sun for desert battles, moody side-lighting for intimate interior meetings, wide expansive landscape shots for naval maneuvers).
