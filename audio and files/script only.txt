Sixty people were locked inside this mall on the first night. By Day 20, you will find proof that one of them is still alive. And that is the least dangerous thing down there.\n\nIt is 8:47 PM on a rainy Thursday. You are in the back corridor of a department store, holding a box cutter and a clipboard, when every phone in the building vibrates at once.\n\nThen the speakers come on. A flat, mechanical voice orders everyone to evacuate immediately. Hundreds of shoppers sprint for the glass doors. The first wave makes it outside. Then steel shutters crash down over every entrance, with sixty people still inside.\n\nOutside, car horns blare and glass shatters. Then the power dies. Four hundred thousand square feet of mall drops into blackness. You are not getting out tonight. And as the screaming outside turns into something worse, you realize you are not getting out tomorrow either.\n\nYou do not waste time on the glass. It is tempered safety glass in steel frames, and breaking it would only open a door to whatever is in that parking lot. Your goal is not escape. It is information.\n\nYou break into the security room, and the cameras are still alive on backup battery. Outside, cars sit with their doors wide open, and dark shapes drift between them. It is already over.\n\nInside, you watch what happens to the sixty. They pound on the shutters. One woman, bitten in the parking lot, collapses by midnight. By one in the morning, she stands up again. The crowd scatters. Eleven people run down the service ramp into the tunnels beneath the mall, and the infected follow. You never learn their names. You never see them again.\n\nAt 6:00 AM on Day 3, the batteries die, and the screens go black. After that, you work by flashlight and memory.\n\nYou choose your base: the sporting goods store on the second floor. One entrance, a steel drop-down grate, no outside windows, and a ladder up to the maintenance catwalks. You wrap the wheels of a stock cart in t-shirts and haul supplies in the dark.\n\nThen the smell starts. By Day 4, the food court freezers are rotting, and the stench creeps through the vents. You seal the corridor with display cases and plastic sheeting, and you do the math that decides everything.\n\nA person working hard needs a gallon of water and about two thousand calories a day. Every plan you make from here on has to survive that number.\n\nBottled water will not carry you sixty days, so you go looking. You set up buckets under the roof drainage pipes and filter every drop through carbon, sand and cotton, then boil it. You drain one of the mall's commercial water heaters, eighty gallons of clean water. And you leave the sprinkler system alone. That water looks like a gift, but it can hold rust inhibitors and antifreeze, and boiling does not remove them. It concentrates them.\n\nYou strip the grocery store of rice, beans, canned chicken and peanut butter, and you hide a second cache, thirty thousand calories and twenty gallons of water, in the first-floor pharmacy. Never keep everything in one place.\n\nThen you write a three-phase plan on a yellow notepad. Phase One: darkness and silence after sundown. Phase Two: the sealed vault. Phase Three: vertical containment. Cut the second floor off from the ground.\n\nYou build Phase Three first. You chain every stairwell door. You unbolt the top landing plates on both central escalators and run steel cable back to a single release pin behind your wall. Pull it, and the top section will drop four feet into the truss pit. You drill four holes in the floor track in front of your gate and keep four steel pins beside it. And you drill one small peephole through the wall, just wide enough to see the floor below.\n\nBy Day 15, you have a hundred gallons of water and two hundred thousand calories. On paper, you are safe. But paper does not listen to the building at night.\n\nOn Day 19, a thunderstorm rolls over the city. At 3:14 in the morning, a sound wakes you. A metallic clank. Then dragging footsteps on the tile below.\n\nYou crawl along the mezzanine on your stomach and look down. In a flash of lightning, you see it: a single infected, stumbling around the fountain. It did not come through the front doors. It came up from the tunnels. One of the sixty. For nineteen days, it has been down there in the dark.\n\nOn Day 20, you corner it in a narrow service doorway and finish it with one strike. But now you have to go down there.\n\nThe air in the basement is cold and smells of wet concrete. As you pass the electrical rooms, your flashlight catches something in the dust. Footprints. Not the drag marks of an infected. Clean boot prints, and next to them an open tin of ration meat and a flashlight with dead batteries. There is almost no dust on the tin. Someone was here less than two days ago.\n\nYou search the tunnels twice and find nothing. Whoever it is, they are hiding.\n\nSo you stop hunting and start trapping. You stretch fishing line ankle-high across every staircase and tunnel door, tied to stacks of empty soda cans.\n\nOn Day 42, at 11:20 at night, the cans rattle. You light a flare and toss it down the escalator bay. A man stands in the red glow, thin and shaking, holding a pipe.\n\n\"Don't shoot,\" he rasps. \"I found the one you put down by the stockroom. Clean strike. I knew somebody was alive up here.\"\n\nYou tell him to drop the pipe. He does. His name is Miller, a delivery driver who was trapped in the loading bay when the shutters fell forty-two days ago. His water ran out three days ago. He talks too much when he is scared, and he is terrified.\n\nYou cannot let him upstairs. A second person doubles your noise and halves your food. You cannot kill a desperate man who has done nothing. So you give him a gated shoe store on the west side of the first floor, a five-gallon jug of boiled water, and two weeks of canned food. And one rule. Stay in the west wing. Do not cross the fountain after dark.\n\nFive days later, you see an orange glow in the west wing. Miller is sitting behind his gate, reading a paperback by candlelight. He says he could not stand the dark. You take his lighter, and you tell him a flame in a glass-fronted mall is a lighthouse. He swears it will never happen again.\n\nYou believe him. Mostly.\n\nOn Day 51, a violent storm hits with sixty-mile-per-hour gusts. At four in the morning, a huge oak branch slams into the central skylight. An eight-foot section of glass falls forty feet into the fountain. The same wind rips your rain buckets off the roof, and your water collection is gone.\n\nThe hole in the roof is worse than the water. It broadcasts every sound in the building into the night, and it vents the smell of people, supplies and rain into the open air.\n\nOn Day 56, the herd arrives.\n\nThey come along the highway, drawn by the flapping insulation and the scent drifting from the dome. At 2:30 on the morning of Day 57, the front doors give way with a sound like a bomb. Steel shrieks as the shutters are peeled up from the concrete. From the second floor, you watch dozens of dark figures pour past the jewelry stores and boutiques toward the center of the building.\n\nYou sprint down to the west wing and hit Miller's gate with your crowbar. \"The front doors are down! Rear stairs, now!\"\n\nMiller grabs his pack, then stops. He left a photo of his daughter on a shelf in the back of the shop. He turns around. You grab his jacket. He tears free, gets the photo, and on the way out he slams into a shattered display case. A sheet of glass opens his calf from knee to ankle. He drops, bleeding onto the tile.\n\nThere is no time to be angry. You haul him up and drag him up the hidden stairs, locking each chained fire door behind you. Below you, the first footsteps echo across the lower concourse.\n\nYou do not try to defend the ground floor. Holding four hundred thousand square feet is suicide. It is time for Phase Three.\n\nYou pull the release pin. The cables snap taut, and both escalators drop four feet into the pit with a crash that rings through the building. They are no longer stairs. They are walls.\n\nYou drag Miller into the sporting goods store, drop the grate, and drive the four pins into the floor. The vault is sealed.\n\nFor thirty-six hours, you sit in total darkness. No candle. No words. Below you, glass explodes, racks crash over, and hundreds of footsteps scrape across the tile.\n\nMiller breaks first. On the second night, he starts whispering to his daughter's photograph. You press a hand over his mouth and put your forehead against his ear. \"If they hear us, we die in this room.\" He nods, shaking. He does not whisper again. But you stay awake, because you now know exactly what a frightened man can cost you.\n\nYou wrap his calf by touch alone. In a building like this, an infection is as deadly as anything outside.\n\nThe herd stays below. You killed every light and sealed your scent behind heavy plastic, and they have no reason to climb. By the evening of Day 58, the sounds begin to thin. On Day 60, you press your eye to the peephole.\n\nThe ground floor is destroyed. Merchandise is scattered across the concourse, and a few dozen stragglers wander through the wreckage. But the second floor is untouched. Your barricades held.\n\nThe cost is real. The herd swept through the pharmacy, and the hidden cache is gone. The roof catchment is gone. Miller cannot put weight on his leg. You count what is left on the yellow notepad: forty-four thousand calories and forty-seven gallons of water. For two people, that is eleven days of food and twenty-three days of water.\n\nYou did not win. You bought time.\n\nMost people look at a huge building and see a fortress.
